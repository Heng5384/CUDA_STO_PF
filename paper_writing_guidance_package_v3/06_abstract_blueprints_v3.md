## Acta-oriented abstract

Ag-alloyed PbTe exhibits a finite-lifetime low-thermal-conductivity state during ageing, but the precipitate population sustaining and closing this state cannot be inferred uniquely because microscopy methods probe different object classes and size windows. We construct experimentally admissible resolved-β microstructure ensembles constrained by Ag inventory, matrix chemistry and observation windows, and propagate their 6–48 h evolution using a globally conserved coherent-elastic phase-field model. Three complete-checkpoint 400³ realizations share the same 512-particle PSD, inventory, physics and numerics but differ in random placement and radius–position assignment. Their final mean radii and interfacial areas span only 3.37% and 4.81% of their case means, whereas the large-tail descriptor `M6` spans 29.56%, revealing spatially sensitive survival pathways. When the evolving full PSD and interfacial area are passed to a frozen V2 transport contract, the predicted interface-driven recovery differs by only approximately 3.3% of its mean signal across the three cases. Thus variables controlling the coarsening pathway are not identical to those controlling its thermal consequence: PSD defines the size hierarchy, spatial organization selects survival, and interfacial-area loss closes the modeled low-κ window. The processing target is therefore the lifetime of the interface-rich state rather than a unique precipitate arrangement.

We develop a versioned, experiment-constrained workflow that represents this ambiguity using admissible resolved-β ensembles, propagates each member with globally conserved coherent-elastic phase-field kinetics, and transfers the resulting full particle-size distributions to a frozen thermal-transport contract. ## 250-word abstract

Ageing Ag-alloyed PbTe produces a finite-lifetime microstructure–transport state: Sheskin's 6 h condition contains a dense Ag-rich nanoparticle population and reduced thermal conductivity, whereas continued ageing to 48 h coarsens the population and accompanies thermal-conductivity recovery. The initial PF-resolved population is nevertheless non-identifiable because APT, SEM/TEM and phase-field segmentation cover different composition, size and connectivity windows. We do not reconstruct a unique experimental 6 h state. Instead, we construct experimentally admissible resolved-β ensembles constrained by total Ag inventory, matrix composition and microscopy-dependent observation windows, and propagate their post-nucleation evolution with a globally conserved coherent-elastic phase-field model.

Three complete-checkpoint 400³ cases use the same `REF_BROAD_METHOD1LIKE` 512-particle PSD, inventory, physics and numerics, varying only particle centers and radius–position assignment. At 48 h they contain 21, 19 and 21 particles, with mean radii of 24.56–25.40 nm, `Sv` of 2.540–2.665×10^6 m^-1 and `M6` of 126.18–168.75 nm^3. Relative case ranges are 3.37%, 4.81% and 29.56%, respectively. Passing the evolving populations to a frozen V2 interface-scattering contract gives 573.15 K recoveries of 4.07–4.21%; the Δκ spread is about 3.3% of its mean. Thus coarsening is spatially sensitive while the modeled interface-driven recovery is spatially robust. PSD defines the size hierarchy, spatial organization selects the survival pathway, and interfacial-area loss closes the modeled low-κ window. Broader experimental recovery remains bounded by unresolved populations and defect-state evolution rather than assigned to a unique missing mechanism. The result shifts processing emphasis from reproducing exact particle coordinates toward preserving the lifetime of the interface-rich population while retaining transport-model and observation uncertainties.
