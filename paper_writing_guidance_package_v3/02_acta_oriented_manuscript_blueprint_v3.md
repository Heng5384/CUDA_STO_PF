# Acta Materialia-oriented manuscript blueprint V3

## Manuscript subject

The subject is a materials mechanism: how an experimentally observed interface-rich low-κ state evolves, why different admissible particle populations follow different coarsening paths, and why path sensitivity need not produce equal thermal sensitivity.

## Recommended structure

1. **Introduction — the finite-lifetime low-κ state.** Open with Sheskin's 6→48 h microstructure–κ trajectory, then state that microscopy windows do not uniquely define the resolved β population. End with the ensemble strategy and pathway–outcome separation.
2. **Experimental and thermodynamic basis.** Define Sheskin's role, data identities, Ag conversion, solvus calibration and the publication-contract boundary. Do not call the four-point fit phase-diagram validation.
3. **Admissible resolved-β ensembles.** State: “We do not reconstruct a unique experimental 6 h precipitate population. Instead, we construct an ensemble of experimentally admissible resolved-β populations constrained by total Ag inventory, matrix composition and microscopy-dependent observation windows.”
4. **Conserved coherent-elastic phase-field propagation.** Give equations, boundary conditions, exclusions, numerical qualification and 6 h conditioning. Absolute nucleation is outside scope.
5. **Observation operator.** Define PF β, APT Ag-rich, unresolved/GP-like and SEM/TEM-visible classes; give blur, threshold, connected components, watershed/neck rule, cutoff and sampling sensitivity. Until implemented, quantitative experiment–PF object validation is blocked.
6. **Results I — admissible initial populations and fixed-PSD controls.** Show inventory/chemistry constraints and Case 001–003 identity. Label numbers `COMPLETE_CHECKPOINT_CONDITIONAL_ANALYSIS` until rerun.
7. **Results II — spatially sensitive coarsening.** Compare survival, PSD tail, `M6`, `Sv` and representative 6→48 h paths. Do not call random seeds designed spatial control.
8. **Results III — spatially robust interface-driven recovery.** Show that Case 001–003 MI recovery is about 4.07–4.21% and that the Δκ spread is approximately 3.3% of its mean. Contrast pathway spread (`M6`) with thermal-signal spread.
9. **Results IV — full PSD and descriptor compression.** Use full PSD as authority; show why `Sv+M6` is better than `Nv+Rmean` or `Sv` alone for tested transport, while `M6` is a tail descriptor rather than a unique κ control.
10. **Discussion I — variables controlling pathway versus consequence.** Build the three-layer statement: PSD defines size hierarchy; spatial organization selects the survival pathway; interfacial-area loss sets the thermal outcome within V2.
11. **Discussion II — wider defect redistribution.** Introduce Yu only after the resolved-β result. Use it to motivate redistribution among GP-like objects, matrix Ag, β, strain and Ag-decorated dislocations.
12. **Discussion III — evidence boundaries and processing tests.** State transport identity, conditional authority, observation gap and three-random-realization limit. Frame thermal history and elastic bias as testable routes.
13. **Conclusions.** End positively: the design target is the lifetime of the interface-rich low-κ state, not unique coordinates. Do not end with model insufficiency.

## Frozen dataset sentence

Sheskin and Yu are used as complementary but non-interchangeable datasets: the former defines the 6–48 h microstructure–thermal-conductivity trajectory, whereas the latter resolves the broader defect redistribution associated with long-term annealing.

## Introduction closing claim

Here we combine experimentally constrained resolved-β ensembles with conserved coherent-elastic phase-field propagation and frozen full-PSD transport to separate variables that select coarsening pathways from those that control their thermal consequence.

## Results closing claim

At fixed initial PSD, precipitate inventory and thermodynamic conditions, random spatial realization substantially altered particle survival and the large-size tail of the evolving PSD, yet changed the predicted interface-driven thermal recovery by only approximately 3–4%. The coarsening pathway is therefore spatially sensitive, whereas the thermal response is comparatively robust. Within the frozen V2 contract, the low-κ window therefore closes primarily through loss of interfacial area.

## Conclusion ending

The identified pathway–outcome separation shifts the processing target from reproducing a unique precipitate arrangement to extending the lifetime of the interface-rich low-κ state. Thermal history and elastic bias are testable routes for that extension, but require dedicated forward simulations and experiments.
